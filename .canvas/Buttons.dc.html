Outline is the workhorse and carries no class of its own — a plain .btn is already the right answer for most actions. Soft exists for the second-most-important action on a screen that has already spent its accent, and never appears beside a solid button.

Gap between icon and label is 6px at every size — the label moves, the gap does not. Radius stays 6px throughout; a large button is not a rounder button.

Icons in buttons

A leading icon names the action. A trailing chevron says the button opens something else — a menu below, a screen to the right — and is drawn 14px so it stays subordinate to the label.

Groups — 2-up and 3-up

Two or more buttons that act on the same object join into one .bgrp. Loose buttons in a row mean the actions are unrelated.

Segmented filter — counts belong in the control

ITF263 across 78 customers. The selected segment takes the soft accent, never the solid — a filter is a view, not a decision, and the counts must add up to the "All" segment or the filter is lying.

Split button

The left half does the thing named on it without asking. The chevron half opens the variants — close and print, close and hold over, close without a Z-read.

The focus ring is 3px of accent-soft on a bordered button, and 2px of surface then 2px of the button's own hue on a filled one — so it reads on white, on blue and on red without a second token. It is never suppressed, and a keyboard-only user must be able to reach Post.

Loading keeps the label and its width — the spinner replaces the icon, the verb turns present-continuous, and the button stays exactly as wide as it was. A button that shrinks mid-post moves everything beside it.

A disabled button always sits above the sentence that would un-disable it. Without it the person has no move left, and the interface has said no without saying why.

The destructive button names the amount it is about to destroy. Its partner is a ghost, not an outline — cancel should be effortless to hit and invisible to scan past.

One solid accent button per screen. Everything else is outline or ghost, and on most records the action a person actually came to do is the bordered one — Export, Print, Save draft. The accent is spent once, on the moment that matters: Post, Approve, Close the till. If two solid buttons ever sit together, one of them is not the primary action.

Danger is never the resting state of a screen. It appears inside a confirmation the person opened, beside the ghost that backs out of it, and it does not hold focus when that confirmation opens.
