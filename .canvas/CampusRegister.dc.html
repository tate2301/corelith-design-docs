A register is marked standing up, on a phone or a tablet, in the two minutes before a lesson starts, by someone holding a book in the other hand. It is the one surface in Corelith that is set to comfortable rather than the default: 44px rows, 40px avatars, a 40px three-state control. Every other rule holds — the same pills, the same hairlines, the same tabular numbers.

Only two things are ever tinted in a register: the control that has been tapped, and the row that has not. A present row is white and silent — thirty-one of them, and any tint on those would drown the three that matter. Absent and late each open one field directly beneath the name, never in a dialogue, because the teacher is still holding the class.

Marking never waits for the line. The control writes to the device, the row keeps the minute it was tapped, and the queue posts in the order it was taken so a learner who was marked late at 07:52 is still late at 07:52 when the record catches up an hour later. Nothing is red: an offline register is not a failure, it is a school in Mutare at half past seven.

Submit is disabled while anything is unmarked, and the two counters say exactly what is missing rather than making the teacher hunt for it — three unmarked rows, one absence without a reason. The unmarked rows are the only ones tinted, so scrolling to them takes no reading. "Mark the rest present" exists because at 07:55 the honest alternative is a teacher tapping present thirty times, and a bulk action that is visible and reversible is safer than one that is neither. After submission the register stops being a form and becomes a record: the marks go grey, and a correction is a request the office answers, because a register is the document a school produces when a learner is reported missing.
