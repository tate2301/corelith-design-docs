Worst offenders (still passing)

--tone-warn on --tone-warn-bg (3.11 : 1) and the green/amber tones on white (3.84 : 1) clear the 3.0 UI-component floor but never quite reach 4.5 body-text AA. Use them for badges and icons only, never for body text. For prose-on-tinted, switch to --tone-success-strong / --tone-danger-strong — both pass AAA on their soft backgrounds.

Semantic roles — when to use what

Five roles, five Do/Don't pairs. Pick the role first; never reach past it to a raw gray.

Brand · the single saturated colour

Reserved for the primary action and the focus ring. One brand button per surface — never two competing.

Do
One brand button. The clear next action.
Don't
Two brand buttons fight for attention. The decline should be ghost or secondary.

Tone-success · completed, healthy

Past-tense state. The work has finished and there's nothing to do.

Do
Posted
Past tense + check icon + tinted background. Three reinforcing signals.
Don't
SAVED!
Success isn't loud. No exclamation, no all-caps, no celebration.

Tone-warn · needs input

Operator action is required, but nothing has broken. Always paired with the count or the next step.

Do
3 fields outstanding
Names the count and the noun. The operator knows what to do.
Don't
Warning
Bare "Warning" tells the operator nothing.

Tone-danger · destructive or failed

Either a destructive action that cannot be silently reversed, or an error the operator needs to address. Never for a low-stakes alert.

Do
Names the specific object. The operator knows exactly what voids.
Don't
Delete what? Always name the noun.

Text-muted · helpers & descriptions

Secondary text that explains the primary text. Never for action labels or anything the operator must read.

Do
Branch
Greendale yard · opens 07:00
Helper text under a strong label. Reads as a hint, not as the answer.
Don't
A primary button label in muted text looks disabled. Use --text-strong.
